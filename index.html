Find the correct angles for every hour line on a horizontal sundial

The angle θ of each hour line from the noon meridian is calculated with:

tan(θ) = sin(latitude) × tan(HA)

where HA is the sun's hour angle — the earth rotates 15° per hour, so 1:00 pm = 15°, 2:00 pm = 30°, etc. Morning hours are the mirror image on the west side of the dial.

Your gnomon (the shadow-casting fin) must be tilted at an angle equal to your latitude, pointing toward true geographic north.

Building your sundial

Step 1 — Draw a straight line on your dial plate. This is the noon line, running due north from the gnomon base.

Step 2 — Using a protractor centered on the gnomon base, measure each dial angle from the noon line. AM hours go to the west (left when facing north); PM hours go to the east (right).

Step 3 — Mount the gnomon so it tilts toward true north at an angle equal to your latitude from the horizontal surface.

Note — Hour lines past 6:00 am / 6:00 pm extend beyond 90° and fall outside the standard dial face. Most horizontal sundials only mark 6 am through 6 pm.
